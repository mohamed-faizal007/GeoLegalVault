Legal documents face two quiet risks. First, a file can be changed after everyone agreed to it, and nobody notices. Second, anyone with a login can upload or approve from anywhere. GeoLegalVault is a prototype that tackles both. Uploads and approvals are allowed only from an approved area. That area is called a geofence, a virtual boundary drawn on a map. Every document is reviewed and approved by different people, so nobody approves their own work. When a version is approved, a fingerprint of the file, called a SHA-256 hash, is written to the Ethereum Sepolia test network. Later, anyone can recompute the fingerprint and see whether the file still matches. This is tamper-evident, which means changes are detected. It does not prevent changes. Everything you will see is the real running system. Where the screen cannot show something, like database records, I label it a supplementary view.
