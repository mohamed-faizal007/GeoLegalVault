Next, review and approval. This uses maker-checker, meaning the person who made a document must not be the one who checks it. A Legal Officer uploads a second document, Doc B, and submits it. A Reviewing Officer, a different person, reviews and passes it. Back as the uploader, the Legal Officer has no Approve button. If they call the server directly anyway, from inside the zone, the answer is: MAKER_CHECKER_VIOLATION. That is a real response, shown in a supplementary view. Now for the main lease. The same Reviewing Officer passes it, and it becomes Pending Approval. Then a Legal Officer who did not upload it clicks Approve. The location is checked first. Approval starts anchoring automatically, and there is no anchor button. The backend's own wallet signs the transaction, never the user. This is a real transaction on the Sepolia test network, the free test version of Ethereum. We wait for it to be included in a block. The status becomes Active. Approval spent a tiny amount of test Ether, about five trillionths of one Ether. Test Ether has no real value.
